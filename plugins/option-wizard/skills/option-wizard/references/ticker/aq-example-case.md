## §7 Step 6 — Final verdict

Decision: **COUNTER** — markup_pp = 3.86 (> 1.5pp threshold, ≤ 5pp threshold).

If PB accepts all 3 lever asks, markup drops to roughly 2.29 − 0.73 − 0.20 ≈
1.36 pp (≤ 1.5pp → institutional band). If PB refuses → recommend REFUSE.

## §8 What this case teaches

1. **KO probability dominates the fair value.** 90.2% KO probability over 12M
   daily-monitored means the structure terminates on average around day 28 of
   252. The trader gets a tiny accumulation window and PB keeps the call value.
2. **Doubling lowers fair yield further than it looks.** The doubling bonus
   ($10,238 of $35,831 short_premium_pv ≈ 29%) is real money — but the tail
   leg ($756) means real-world chains with thicker deep-OTM premiums will move
   the lever-3 sign in the opposite direction.
3. **Chain-priced legs avoid the "vol-markup" trap.** Reading mids directly
   from the chain (instead of recomputing via BSM with PB-quoted vols) means
   we never inherit PB's vol assumption — the markup is purely the gap between
   what PB pays for hedging and what they charge the client.
4. **Counter-offer math beats vibes.** Without this framework, a 4% yield on a
   stock you'd own anyway sounds OK; the framework shows it's 28× the fair
   yield (0.14%). The counter ask brings it to institutional territory.
5. **Lead with low-difficulty levers; use doubling/KO as walk-away leverage.**
   Tenor and obs-freq changes (difficulty 1–2) are easy negotiation wins;
   doubling and KO push-out (difficulty 3.5–4.5) are the asks you escalate to
   if the easy levers don't get you under 1.5pp.
